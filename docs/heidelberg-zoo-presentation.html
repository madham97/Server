Why Automate Monitoring?

Manual monitoring is difficult:

  • Animals are active at unpredictable hours — often overnight
  • Large areas to cover with limited staff time
  • Reviewing hours of footage is slow and expensive
  • Human presence can disturb animal behaviour

What automation gives us:

  • 24/7 coverage with no ongoing effort
  • Instant detection, no footage to review manually
  • Objective, timestamped records
  • A system that improves over time as it sees more data
Our goal: Deploy a low-cost, always-on camera system that automatically automatically detects rodents in field footage — and gets smarter with every image it sees.

The Research Innovation — Multimodal Detection

Most wildlife monitoring systems use a single camera type. We use two cameras simultaneously, pointing at the same scene:


📷
Infrared (NIR) camera
Captures detail, texture and shape in low-light conditions. Works like a standard camera but sensitive beyond visible light — effective at dusk, dawn, and in dimly lit enclosures.
🌡️
Thermal camera
Detects heat signatures rather than reflected light. Works in complete darkness, through light vegetation, and is unaffected by shadows or camouflage. A warm animal is always visible.

Why combine them?

  • Thermal finds the animal — even in the dark or when hidden
  • Infrared provides the detail needed to identify the species
  • Each modality compensates for the other's weaknesses
  • Together they should outperform either camera alone

Our research question:

Does fusing infrared and thermal images improve rodent detection accuracy compared to using either sensor alone? And under which conditions does each modality contribute most?

The two software components are:

  • Server — runs on our lab machine with a GPU

GSM/Cellular HATSends images from both cameras over the mobile network
SIM card2G data — works wherever there is signal

Two recording modes:

  • Motion-triggered — activates only when movement is detected (saves data costs)
  • Fixed interval — takes a photo every N seconds around the clock
Both cameras capture the same scene simultaneously. Every motion event produces a matched infrared/thermal image pair — giving the AI two complementary views of the same moment. The device starts automatically on power-up and restarts itself if anything fails.

Managing the Device

Two ways to control the camera — no physical intervention needed for day-to-day adjustments:

💬 SMS — remote control from anywhere
Text the SIM card number to reconfigure the camera in real time. No internet needed.
e.g. SET motion_threshold=0.03 → more sensitive
e.g. STATUS → device replies with current settings
📶 WiFi — local access on-site
When physically at the location, connect directly over WiFi to inspect logs, adjust config files, or update software — no cellular needed

What Happens After an Image Arrives

📥 Image received & stored
↓
🤖 AI runs automatically — detects objects, draws bounding boxes
↓
👁️ Human reviews & corrects labels — using our annotation tool
↓
📦 Labelled images packaged as a training dataset
↓
🏋️ New AI model trained — only deployed if it meets quality threshold
This loop runs continuously. Every new image from the zoo makes the model a little smarter.

Quality gate: A newly trained model is only deployed if it achieves a minimum detection accuracy score (mAP ≥ 0.3). Otherwise the existing model stays live. Previous models are always archived — we can roll back at any time.

The AI Detection Model

We use YOLOv8 — a widely used object detection model in research and industry.

What it does:

  • Analyses each image and draws bounding boxes around detected objects
  • Assigns a class label: rat
  • Reports a confidence score for every detection

The experiment:

  1. Train and evaluate the model on infrared images alone
  2. Train and evaluate on thermal images alone
  3. Fuse both modalities — does combining them outperform either alone?
  4. Identify which conditions (night, day, occlusion) each modality handles best

Annotation Tool — How We Label Images

A browser-based tool — no installation required:

  • Opens any uploaded image in the browser
  • Researcher draws rectangles around rodents — or marks human presence to help the model learn the distinction
  • Assigns a label: rat or human
  • Click Submit — label is saved instantly
  • If no target is visible, submit an empty image (useful as a negative example)

This is how raw camera images become training data that makes the AI smarter with every batch.

What Would Help Us Most

We would love your input on two things that would meaningfully broaden what the system can learn:

📐
1. Different camera angles within the enclosure
All our data so far comes from a single fixed viewpoint. Additional angles — overhead, ground-level, side-on — would help the model generalise beyond what it has seen, making detections more reliable in varied conditions.
🗺️
2. Known rodent hotspots outside the enclosures
Rodents tend to use areas like burrow sites, feed stores, service corridors, and sheltered perimeter zones. If your team knows of any such spots around the zoo, those would be ideal places to extend the monitoring — and would give us a much richer picture of rodent behaviour beyond the enclosure itself.
